The best travel itinerary apps in 2026

There's no single best itinerary app: it depends on whether you want help planning, a place to store bookings, or a map to plan on together. Here's how three popular options compare.

Download on theApp Store
This page is written by the team behind ViaHub, so we're not neutral. We've tried to be fair to the other apps: prices and features are as listed on their websites or in public reviews in October 2026, and they change, so check the latest before you decide.

What to look for in an itinerary app

  • Planning help: does it suggest things to do and build a day-by-day plan, or do you do all the research yourself?
  • Booking organisation: can it collect flights, stays and reservations in one timeline?
  • Costs: does it estimate the trip budget and track and split expenses?
  • Group travel: can companions see and work on the same trip?
  • Platforms and price: which devices it runs on, and what's behind a paywall.

ViaHub: best for planning a trip from scratch

ViaHub starts before you've booked anything. The AI trip planner suggests things to do, estimates flights, stays, food and transport for your group and drafts a day-by-day plan, including multi-city trips. Once you book, the plan becomes a real trip with a timeline, shared finances, a travel checklist and optional route logging.

Good to know: ViaHub is Apple-only (iPhone, iPad and Mac), so it's not the right pick if your group travels with Android phones.

TripIt: best for organising bookings you already have

TripIt is a long-established itinerary organiser. Its core trick is building your itinerary from confirmation emails, and the free version syncs with your calendar and lets you share plans. TripIt Pro ($49 a year) adds real-time flight alerts, fare refund monitoring, check-in reminders and airport navigation, which frequent flyers love.

It's less about deciding what to do: if you want planning help, it's a weaker fit. Read our ViaHub vs TripIt comparison.

Wanderlog: best for planning places on a map together

Wanderlog combines an itinerary with a map, and its free plan includes live collaboration, imported reservations and expense tracking. Pro (around $40 a year) adds offline access, route optimisation, Gmail scanning and unlimited AI assistance.

It's a strong choice for detailed research on a map. Read our ViaHub vs Wanderlog comparison.

So which one should you pick?

  • You don't know where to start, or want a budget before booking: ViaHub.
  • Your trips are already booked and you fly a lot: TripIt.
  • You love pinning places on a map with friends, across devices: Wanderlog.

Many travellers combine apps. Still, the fewer places your trip lives in, the less you'll be searching your inbox at the airport.

Is there an itinerary app for group trips?
