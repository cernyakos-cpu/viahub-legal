How to Estimate Your Trip Budget Before You Book

Knowing roughly what a trip will cost before you book is the difference between a relaxed holiday and an overdrawn account. Here is a simple way to build a realistic travel budget.

By the ViaHub team · 30 October 2026 · 6 min read

Break the trip into five buckets

  • Transport to and from: flights, trains or fuel.
  • Accommodation: usually the largest single cost; multiply by nights.
  • Food and drink: estimate per person per day for your travel style.
  • Local transport: airport transfers, public transport passes, taxis, rental car.
  • Activities and extras: tickets, tours, souvenirs, tips.

Use a daily budget for the small stuff

Food, local transport and small purchases are easier to budget as a daily amount per person. Multiply by the number of days, then compare with what you actually spend and adjust. Eating mostly at cafes and markets costs far less than three restaurant meals a day, so be honest about your style.

Add a buffer

Add about ten to fifteen percent for surprises: a missed connection, a rainy-day museum, a pricier dinner. If you never use it, you simply return home with spare money.

Track real spending on the trip

Log expenses as you go. If you travel with others, track shared costs in the same place so settling up is painless. See how to split travel expenses with friends.

Ways to lower the cost without lowering the fun

  • Travel in shoulder season, just before or after the peak.
  • Choose accommodation with a kitchen or breakfast included.
  • Book flights with flexible dates and nearby airports.
  • Mix one splurge with several cheap, great experiences.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a travel budget?

Add up transport, accommodation, food, local transport and activities, then add a buffer of about ten to fifteen percent.

How much should I budget per day for a trip?

It depends heavily on the destination and style, so estimate from real prices for your destination. A cost estimator gives a useful starting point.
