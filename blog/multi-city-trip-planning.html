Multi-City Trip Planning: Route, Nights and Transport

Visiting several cities in one trip multiplies the fun and the complexity. Here is how to choose the route order, split your nights sensibly and connect the stops without losing days to transport.

By the ViaHub team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Choose a route that avoids backtracking

Draw your cities on a map and look for a line or loop. If you fly in and out of different airports (an open-jaw route), you never need to return to the start. Backtracking is the single biggest time-waster in multi-city travel. Put the cities that are hardest to reach at the beginning or the end of the trip, not in the middle.

How many nights in each city?

As a rule of thumb, give a major city at least two full days; three if you want museums, food and a day trip. Smaller towns often work with one or two nights. Every move between cities costs about half a day in practice, so a 7-day trip with four cities is mostly travelling. Fewer stops, longer stays is usually the better trip.

ViaHub's planner splits your nights across the stops automatically and lets you shift a night from one city to the next while keeping the trip length fixed.

Pick transport between cities

  • Train: city-centre to city-centre, no security queues. Often best under three to four hours.
  • Flight: worth it for long hops, but add airport time at both ends.
  • Bus: usually cheapest, and sometimes the only direct option.
  • Car: flexible for rural routes, expensive and awkward in cities.

Compare total door-to-door time, not just the journey itself.

Put the stops on a timeline

Once the order is decided, put every leg on one timeline: departure time, arrival time, booking number. The ViaHub travel timeline shows all legs of a trip in order, so you always know what is next. For an example, see our Oslo and Copenhagen itinerary.

Handle accommodation per stop

Book each city's accommodation for the nights you will actually stay, close to the station if you arrive by train. Avoid changing hotel more than needed: every check-out and check-in eats half a day.

Frequently asked questions

How many cities can I visit in a week?

Two or three is comfortable for a week. Four or more is possible but means most days include travel.

What is the best way to order the cities on my route?

Pick the order that follows a rough line or loop on the map, and avoid backtracking. A route optimiser can find the shortest order for you.
