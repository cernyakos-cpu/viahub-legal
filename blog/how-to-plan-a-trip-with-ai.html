How to Plan a Trip With AI, and What It Still Gets Wrong

An AI trip planner can turn 'a week in Portugal in June' into a draft itinerary, cost estimate and packing outlook in minutes. Here is how to get the most out of it and where you still need to double-check.

By the ViaHub team · 30 October 2026 · 6 min read

What an AI trip planner is good at

AI is excellent at the blank-page problem. Give it your origin, destination, dates and travel style, and it proposes things to do, a sensible number of nights per city, rough costs and a day-by-day outline. It also saves hours of cross-referencing blogs and reviews.

Give it good inputs

  • Origin and destination(s): include every city you want to visit.
  • Dates: exact dates or a flexible month and length.
  • Who is travelling: number of travellers, and whether there are young children.
  • Style: budget, mid-range or comfortable stays, and whether breakfast matters.
  • Interests: food, museums, beaches, hiking, nightlife.

The more specific you are, the less generic the plan. The ViaHub AI trip planner asks for exactly these inputs, suggests things to do first and lets you pick your favourites before it builds the plan.

Turn the draft into your trip

Treat the AI result as a first draft. Drag activities between days, remove what you do not like, add your own ideas and check the cost estimate against your budget. A planner that keeps the result as an editable trip, rather than a block of text, saves you from copying it into notes.

What AI can still get wrong

  • Opening hours and prices change. Treat costs as estimates and confirm before you book.
  • Visa and entry rules. Always verify with official government sources.
  • Small or local places. Check recent reviews before building a day around one restaurant.
  • Travel times. Verify transport connections on the operator's own site.
AI is a planner, not a booking agent. Use it to decide faster, then confirm the facts that matter.
